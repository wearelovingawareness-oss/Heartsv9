Whai wahi mai

Be Part of Our Kaupapa

Step into the H.E.A.R.T.S. whānau and the healing presence of our horses — beings who listen with their whole bodies, steady our wairua, and gently guide us back to ourselves.

Whether through your koha, your time as a volunteer, your tautoko, or simply by sharing our story, you become part of a community restoring right relationship. Together, we strengthen the mauri of people, horses, whānau, and whenua — creating spaces where healing, belonging, and hope can emerge.

Why Give

H.E.A.R.T.S. Charitable Trust is a registered charitable organisation (CC51224) under the Charities Act 2005. Your donation directly supports equine-assisted healing for rangatahi Māori, survivors of trauma, and those in crisis.

Every contribution helps us keep our herd, maintain our whenua, and bring healing to those who need it most.

Volunteering

Join Us on the Kāpiti Coast

Nau mai, haere mai — come and connect with us in this kaupapa on the beautiful Kāpiti Coast. Just north of Ōtaki, HEARTS rests between the Tararua Ranges and Kāpiti Island, a place where the winds, the whenua, and our horses hold space for healing, connection, and growth.

This is a living, breathing environment — sustainable, supportive, and holistic. Where both horses and humans can thrive. If you’re drawn to meaningful mahi, value whanaungatanga, and want to contribute to something bigger than yourself, we’d love to welcome you into the HEARTS whānau.

Nga Tangata

We’re Looking For People Who…

  • ✓ Live in Aotearoa
  • ✓ Hold a valid NZ visa (if applicable)
  • ✓ Have an interest or experience with horses
  • ✓ Can communicate in conversational English
  • ✓ Bring a positive, can-do attitude
  • ✓ Treat people, horses, and whenua with respect
  • ✓ Have their own transport
  • ✓ Have their own accommodation and meals

Experience with recycled materials, composting, sustainable building, or regenerative land practices is an advantage — but not essential. What matters most is your wairua, your willingness, and your respect for the kaupapa.

Outcomes Research

Adolescent Sexual Abuse & Trauma — EAGALA Model

Researchers found significant improvements in psychological distress and functioning after EAGALA Model intervention for adolescents who experienced sexual abuse. Journal of Child and Family Studies, 2013

Psychiatric Patients & Violent Behaviour

Hospitalized patients randomly assigned to EAGALA Model equine-assisted psychotherapy showed the largest decrease in violent behaviour compared to other interventions. Psychiatric Services, 2014

Student Stress Reduction

Students receiving EAGALA Model stress management showed significant reductions in stress, performing as well as other modalities including yoga and mindfulness. Journal of Creativity in Mental Health, 2017
